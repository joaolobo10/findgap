# The Understanding Ladder

The full protocol for climbing the ladder on one topic: what to ask at each rung, what counts as passing, and what a stall means. The rung where the learner stalls is the diagnosis.

## How to run it

- **One topic at a time, one rung per message.** Never stack two rungs in one question. The learner should always know what is being asked.
- **From memory.** Ask the learner to answer without looking anything up. Say it once, kindly: "No notes for this one — it's how we find out what's really there."
- **Never show the answer before the attempt.** Not as a hint, not as an example, not in the question itself.
- **Climb until the first clear stall,** or until the topic's target rung (from the knowledge map). Then stop, give short feedback, log, and move to the next topic.
- **Budget:** about 5–10 minutes per topic. A ladder that drags is a sign the topic is too broad; narrow it.

### Pass, partial, stall

Every rung is judged as one of:

- **Pass:** meets the rung's criteria below.
- **Partial:** on the right track but incomplete or imprecise. Give **one** more chance: rephrase or narrow the question, without revealing content. Two partials in a row count as a stall.
- **Stall:** wrong, empty, "I don't know", or two partials.

**Scaffolds** (rephrasing, narrowing, offering two options to choose from) are allowed, but a rung passed with a scaffold is logged as *passed with scaffold*. That makes the topic **fragile**, not mastered.

### Allowed shortcuts

- **Fast lane:** if a rung 1 explanation is already precise and causal, merge rung 2 into a single hard probe.
- **Start higher:** if the breadth quiz already showed correct, confident answers at the Apply level on this topic, you may start at rung 4. Run rung 1 later if the goal requires explaining (teaching, interviews, oral exams).
- **Tacit knowledge:** if the learner stalls at Explain but says they use it in practice, run rung 4 (Apply) before concluding. If they pass Apply, log *can do, can't explain*. This matters only when the goal requires explaining.

## The rungs

### Rung 0 — Claim

**Goal.** Record what the learner believes before any evidence (E7, E8).

**Ask.** "Before we start: from 1 to 4, how well do you know [topic]?" Anchors: 1 = barely know it, 2 = studied it but shaky, 3 = could explain most of it, 4 = could teach it.

**Never** comment on the rating. It is a baseline, not a judgment.

### Rung 1 — Explain

**Goal.** Can the learner retrieve and articulate the idea, or only recognize it? (E1, E5)

**Ask.** "Without looking anything up, explain [topic] in your own words, as if to a colleague who missed that class."

**Pass.**
- The core idea is correct.
- The key components are present (what it is, what it does, how its parts relate).
- No major errors.
- For Remember-level topics: the required facts or definitions are recalled accurately.

**Stall means → Recognition only.** It feels familiar, but it cannot be retrieved.

**Variant: explain it like I'm a child (E12).** Play a curious, sharp 10-year-old. Ask at least three follow-ups in character: "Why?", "What does that word mean?", "What happens if…?", "But how does it *know* to do that?". The learner must avoid jargon or explain any jargon they use.
- **Pass:** a child could follow it and it stays accurate. Simplifying is fine; saying something false is not.
- Stay in character, but drop it for a moment if the learner gets lost.

### Rung 2 — Probe

**Goal.** Is there a causal model behind the words, or just a memorized description? (E5, E6, E7)

**Ask** 2–4 questions aimed at the links in *their own* explanation. Mix the types:

| Type | Template |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | "Why does [A] lead to [B]?" |
| Boundary | "When does this *not* hold?" |
| Consequence | "What happens if [component] is removed, doubled or delayed?" |
| Precision | "What exactly do you mean by [term they used]?" |

**Pass.** The core causal links hold up. "I'm not sure" on a genuine edge case is acceptable if the core stays intact.

**Then re-rate.** Ask for the 1–4 rating again. A drop of 2 or more points is an *illusion of explanatory depth*: log it, and mention it gently ("This is very common — explaining it in detail is exactly what reveals it").

**Stall means → Surface knowledge.** The words are there, but the causal model is not.

### Rung 3 — Analogy

**Goal.** Is the understanding flexible, or tied to one framing? (E13)

**Ask, in two steps:**
1. "Give me an everyday example or analogy for [topic], something from outside this field."
2. "Where does your analogy break? What does it get wrong about [topic]?"

**Pass.** Both conditions:
- The analogy preserves the **core relationship**, not just a surface feature.
- The learner names at least one meaningful limit.

**If they can't produce an analogy:** offer two candidate analogies, one sound and one subtly flawed in a way that reflects a common misconception. Ask which fits better and why. Getting this right counts as *passed with scaffold*.

**Skip** this rung for Remember-level topics (pure facts and definitions).

**Stall means → Rigid model.** The understanding works only in the framing where it was learned.

### Rung 4 — Apply

**Goal.** Can the learner *use* it, not just talk about it? (E1, E16)

**Ask** a problem of a familiar type, similar to what their materials and the criterion contain: a calculation, a short case, a small task, a snippet to write, a decision to make. **Collect confidence (1–4) before revealing whether the answer is right.**

**Pass.** The approach and the result are correct. A minor slip is acceptable if the learner catches it when asked to check their work.

**Stall means → Declarative only.** They know *about* it, but cannot do it.

### Rung 5 — Discriminate

**Goal.** Does the learner know *when* the concept applies, and can they recognize misuse? (E4, E15, E16)

**Ask** one of two formats, or both for high-weight topics:
- **Which applies?** A situation where 2–3 confusable concepts are plausible: "Which one fits here, and why not the others?"
- **Spot the error.** A worked solution, argument or decision containing one planted error that reflects a known misconception: "Something here is wrong. Find it, explain why, and fix it."

Collect confidence before revealing.

**Pass.**
- They make the right choice and justify it with the distinguishing feature, not by elimination or a hunch.
- Or they find the planted error and explain why it is wrong.

**Stall means → Doesn't know when.** They can do it when told to, but cannot recognize when it applies.

### Rung 6 — Transfer

**Goal.** Does the knowledge work outside the format it was studied in? (E13, E14)

**Ask** a case that changes at least one dimension from how they studied:
- a different context or field;
- a different format (data, diagram, dialogue, real-world mess instead of a textbook statement);
- a different purpose (advising a client, deciding under constraints, explaining to a non-expert).

Make it *unfamiliar*, not *harder*. The difficulty should come from recognizing the principle, not from extra complexity. Collect confidence before revealing.

**Pass.** They recognize the underlying principle and adapt it sensibly. If they need a cue ("this relates to [topic]"), log *transfer with cue*, which counts as partial.

**Stall means → Inert knowledge.** It works only in the format where it was learned.

### Rung 7 — Retain

**Goal.** Is it still there later? (E3, E8, E9, E11)

**When.** In a later session, at least one day after the last diagnosis, with the gap scaled to the deadline (the roadmap sets the date). The learner must not review beforehand.

**Ask** a *new variant* (never the identical question) at the topic's target rung. Collect confidence first.

**Pass → confirmed.** This is the only way a topic becomes *confirmed*.

**Mandatory here:** every dangerous gap (a wrong answer rated 3–4) gets re-tested at this rung.

**Stall means → Not consolidated.** Known that day, gone later.

## After a stall

Give feedback in 2–3 lines, kind and specific, then move on:

> Not quite. [What was off, in one sentence.] The key idea is [correct idea, 1–2 sentences], and it matters because [one line]. I've noted it, and we'll come back to it.

Do not teach the topic in depth here. Deep work belongs in the roadmap.

## Gap types → what the roadmap should do

| Stalled at | Gap type | Roadmap focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Explain | Recognition only | Free-recall practice with feedback (E1) |
| 2 Probe | Surface knowledge | Self-explanation and why-questions on the mechanism (E5, E6) |
| 3 Analogy | Rigid model | Comparing cases that share the structure (E13) |
| 4 Apply | Declarative only | Practice problems with corrective feedback (E1, E10) |
| 5 Discriminate | Doesn't know when | Interleaved practice with confusable cases (E4); misconception contrast (E15) |
| 6 Transfer | Inert knowledge | Varied practice across contexts and formats (E13, E14) |
| 7 Retain | Not consolidated | Spaced retrieval (E3) |

## Status assignment

After the ladder on a topic, set its status in the gap log:

| Result | Status |
|---|---|
| Reached the target rung, confidence 3–4, no scaffold | **mastered** (still needs rung 7 to become *confirmed*) |
| Reached the target rung, but with confidence 1–2 or with a scaffold | **fragile** |
| Stalled, with confidence 1–2 | **known gap** |
| Stalled, with confidence 3–4 at the stall or a rung-0 claim of 3–4 | **dangerous gap** |
| Passed rung 7 | **confirmed** |

Log the highest rung passed, the rung of the stall, the gap type, the last confidence and the status. Also log every wrong answer rated 3–4 as its own row under *Dangerous gaps and errors*, with a re-test date.

## Edge cases

- **"I don't know."** Treat it as a stall, without penalty. Thank them: an honest "I don't know" is better data than a guess.
- **Looked it up.** If an answer reads like copied text, ask for the same idea in different words, or move to rung 2. Do not accuse.
- **Frustration.** Normalize it ("finding gaps is the whole point; this is the useful part"). Offer a short break, or switch to a topic they are likely to pass, then come back.
- **You are not sure the learner is wrong.** In niche or high-stakes topics, if you cannot be confident in the correct answer, say so. Name the source that would settle it, and do not mark the answer as wrong.
- **The learner disputes a correction.** Engage with their argument. If they are right, say so plainly and update the log. If they are not, explain why once, cite the criterion or a source, and move on.
