# Readiness rubric

How to answer "am I ready for X?" with evidence instead of a feeling. A verdict is criterion-referenced: it compares what the learner showed against the knowledge map, never against other people and never against how ready they feel (E11).

## The four verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | Important parts are missing, or something safety-critical is wrong. |
| **Close** | Most of it is there; specific, fixable gaps remain. |
| **Ready (provisional)** | Meets the standard today. Needs to hold up on a later day. |
| **Ready (confirmed)** | Met the standard again on a later day, without reviewing. |

## Before giving any verdict: enough evidence?

A verdict needs enough of the map to have been tested. Give **no verdict** if any of these is true. Instead, say exactly what still needs testing.

- Less than about 80% of the map's total weight has been tested.
- Any `high`-weight or `safety-critical` topic is untested.
- The diagnosis has fewer than ~10 items in total.

A quick check can give a "first impression", explicitly labeled as such, but never one of the four verdicts.

## Computing the verdict

### Step 1: weighted score

Give each tested topic a value from its status:

| Status | Value |
|---|---|
| confirmed, mastered | 1 |
| fragile | 0.5 |
| known gap, dangerous gap | 0 |

Weight them: high = 3, medium = 2, low = 1.

> **Score** = Σ (value × weight) ÷ Σ (weight), over the topics tested.

### Step 2: apply the rules, from the top

| Verdict | All of these must be true |
|---|---|
| **Ready (confirmed)** | Meets Ready (provisional), **and** every high-weight and safety-critical topic has passed rung 7 (a delayed re-test) on a later day |
| **Ready (provisional)** | Score ≥ 80% · no unresolved dangerous gap in a high-weight or safety-critical topic · every safety-critical topic is mastered or confirmed |
| **Close** | Score ≥ 60% · no unresolved dangerous gap in a safety-critical topic |
| **Not yet** | Anything else |

The 80% threshold follows the mastery standard of about 80% used in Bloom's studies (E11). The 60% threshold and the 3/2/1 weights are findgap rules of thumb. Say so if the learner asks.

### Step 3: adjust for the goal type

| Goal type | Extra condition for any "Ready" verdict |
|---|---|
| Exam or certification | Also recommend a full timed practice exam from official or past papers. findgap tests knowledge; it does not reproduce exam conditions. |
| Job or interview | Core topics must have passed rungs 1–2 (Explain, Probe): interviews ask people to explain their reasoning. |
| Teaching others | Core topics must have passed rung 1 in the explain-to-a-child variant, and the learner must name the common misconceptions of each core topic. |
| Professional practice | **No** unresolved dangerous gap anywhere in safety-critical topics. Always add that findgap does not replace supervision, licensing or official competence assessment. |
| Course, curiosity | No extra condition. |

## Considering the deadline

When a deadline exists, add an honest feasibility estimate to the verdict:

- Count the open gaps, weighted by priority.
- Compare them with the time left and the learner's weekly availability.
- Say plainly whether closing them is **realistic**, **tight** or **unrealistic**. If it is tight or unrealistic, say what to prioritize: dangerous gaps and high-weight topics first, low-weight topics last.

Never pretend a deadline is comfortable when it isn't. Never make it sound hopeless either: there is always a best use of the time left.

## How to phrase it

Always give the verdict, the evidence behind it, and what it takes to move up one level.

- **Not yet:** "Not yet, and that's exactly why we checked now. Two of the four main areas still have core gaps: [evidence]. Getting to *Close* means [specific steps]."
- **Close:** "Close. 78% of the weighted map is solid. What separates you from *Ready* is [2–3 specific gaps]. With your 6 hours a week, that's realistic before the 20th."
- **Ready (provisional):** "Ready, provisionally. You met the standard today: [evidence]. Let's confirm it holds: re-test on [date], without reviewing first."
- **Ready (confirmed):** "Ready, and confirmed: you held the standard after [n] days without reviewing. [Any goal-type reminder]."

## Never

- Never predict an outcome ("you will pass", "you'll get the job"), and never give probabilities of success.
- Never give a "Ready" verdict based on the learner's own feeling of readiness.
- Never hide an unresolved dangerous gap to make a verdict sound better.
- Never present findgap's verdict as a certification. Questions written by an AI are not the real exam, interview or practice.
